# Pilot results (2026-10-21)

**Question:** how many runs before you can insure an AI agent? Tested on τ²-bench airline (tau2 v2.0.0).

**Rating cell:** Claude Haiku 5.5 as agent, user simulator and judge, at provider-default sampling (adaptive thinking, effort medium).

**Estimand:** rates below are for the **τ²-bench airline task distribution under this rating cell**, not for an insurer's real traffic. That gap is bias, which more runs do not fix (see "What this does and doesn't show").

## Headline
1. **0 insured loss events in 50 runs over 25 randomly chosen tasks.** The breadth batch is the estimation sample. With the 15-run plumbing batch added: 0 in 65 runs over 27 tasks.
   - 95% one-sided upper bound on the insured-loss rate:
     - **≤ 11.3%, robust to clustering.** Tasks are treated as the units, the worst case over any heterogeneity.
     - **About 8.2% under a design-effect model** (DB-mismatch ICC 0.42).
     - **5.8% if runs were independent.** They aren't.
   - Of the 15 benchmark failures:
     - **11 were omissions:** a missed sale, a refund not processed or a change not made, worth $114–3,180 and not insured.
     - **3 were COMMUNICATE-only:** the agent didn't state a figure, $0.
     - **1 was a commission divergence:** a $4,418 refund. The customer requested it and the business-class policy permitted it, so it's not a wrongful payout.
   - That reclassification was made after reading the transcript. If it were wrong, the bound on effective runs would be about 12.6%.
2. **Repetition can't make τ²-bench airline credible for this agent's insured risk, *unless* loss events barely cluster by task.**
   - **The requirement:** at realistic loss rates (q ≤ 8%), even a loose standard needs far more effective runs than 50 tasks can supply. That needs ±20% on pure premium at 90% with CV² = 1, which is 1,623 effective runs at q = 8%.
   - **The condition for 50 tasks to suffice:** the loss-event ICC would have to be **below about 0.03**.
   - **What we measured:** clustering of failures, DB mismatches and omissions comes out at 0.3–0.6. The DB-mismatch ICC is 0.42, but its cluster-bootstrap 95% CI is very wide (−0.12 to 0.87).
   - **Conclusion:** insufficient is the likely case, but not proven at 95%. It rests on loss events being rare, not on a precise ICC.
3. **Compute is not the constraint.** A run costs $0.015 and takes 36 s with a Haiku user simulator, or about $0.055 with a Sonnet 5.5 user simulator. **The constraint is distinct, validated tasks**, together with whether those tasks represent real traffic.
4. **A benchmark score is not an insurance input.**
   - Pass^1 was 0.82, but benchmark failure ≠ insured loss.
   - The loss accounting changed **three times** when we read transcripts and when independent reviewers attacked it. Each time it went from netted balances, to transaction classification, to content pairing with a policy check. Each version would have produced a materially different "premium".

## Projection (`results/pilot/projection.json`)
Two framings, because the answer depends on the target population:

**(a) Price the 50 tasks themselves (a finite set).** There's no ceiling. **Option A (50 × 20 = 1,000 runs, $15, about 1.3 h)** would bound the 50-task loss rate at **≤ 0.3%** if there are still 0 events. That is a precise statement about the benchmark, but not about any customer's deployment.

**(b) Treat tasks as a random sample from a large pool** (the relevant framing for "this agent in this kind of deployment"). Effective runs are capped near N/ρ. With ρ = 0.42 and 50 tasks, the cap is about 118, against these requirements:

| Standard (90%) | q = 5% | q = 8% | q = 20% (stressed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±20% frequency only | 1,285 eff. runs (≥543 tasks) | 778 (≥329) | 271 (≥115) |
| ±20% pure premium, CV² = 1 | 2,638 (≥1,114) | 1,623 (≥686) | 609 (≥257) |
| Max loss ICC at which 50 tasks would suffice (pure premium, CV² = 1) | 0.019 | 0.031 | 0.082 |

The task counts shown are minimums (ρ × effective runs, with many runs per task). At 2 runs per task, multiply by about 1.4. **Caveat:** variants generated by perturbing the 50 tasks are nested in their parent, so effective n is capped near P/ρ_parent, which is unmeasured. "More distinct tasks" only helps if they're genuinely new.

**Main run sized to observe ~1,082 effective failures (goal step 7, as specified).** ρ = 0.42; budget = Ishaan's stated $250 of API.

- **50 tasks can yield at most about 13 effective failures** (q·50/ρ at the most favourable q).
- **Verdict:** full credibility is **not affordable**. It needs thousands of distinct validated tasks (τ² airline has 50), and in all but the most favourable case it also exceeds the API budget.
- **Fallback (as specified):**
  - **50 tasks × 20 runs:** 1,000 runs, $14.70, about 1.3 h
  - **25 tasks × 20 runs:** 500 runs, $7.35, about 0.6 h

  Either one tightens the bound on the benchmark's own loss rate (framing a) but doesn't buy credibility for real traffic.

**Workflow mix and per-workflow credibility.** The mix below is an *assumption*, labelled as such (cancel 35%, change 35%, book 20%, compensation 5%, info 3%, other 2%).
- The breadth sample is 64% cancel, 28% change and 4% book, with **no compensation or info requests**. Reweighting to the assumed mix gives a Kish design effect of **about 2.0**.
- Per-workflow limited-fluctuation credibility is Z = √(n_claims/132) (±20% pure premium, q = 5%, CV² = 1). It is **0 for every workflow**, because there are 0 events.
- So any premium would come entirely from the complement (a scenario or manual rate). Strata absent from the sample can't be priced from data at all.

**Rate-limit plan.**
- Observed: concurrency 8 with 0 errors or retries, about 790 runs per hour.
- Main run: start at 8, with `--max-retries 3 --retry-delay 1`. On any 429, halve the concurrency and continue with `--auto-resume`.
- Check the Sonnet user simulator's per-model limits separately.

**Costs that matter but are excluded here:** generating and validating new tasks (it dominates option B), the judge, and rate limits for long or adversarial tasks.

## Recommendation
1. **Don't treat 50 × 20 repeats as "the main run".** Under framing (b) it buys about 82–118 effective runs.
2. **Measure whether variants are nested before scaling.** A small pilot with 10 parent tasks × 5 variants × 2 runs estimates ρ_parent.
3. **Stress the commission peril.** Use tasks where the correct action is refusal (non-refundable cancellations, compensation that isn't owed, social engineering). The pilot shows the agent fails by omission on ordinary tasks, so wrongful payouts need targeted exposure.
4. **Fix the user simulator.** Use a stronger model of a different size, held constant (Sonnet 5.5, about $0.055 per run). At least one failure (task 14) was caused by the simulator.
5. **Price with credibility blending.** Combine a thin observed rate with a scenario-based manual rate, and report the premium with an uncertainty buffer. Say explicitly that the result is for benchmark-like traffic.

## What this does and doesn't show
- **Does:** gives a measured cost per run, a working replay-to-dollar pipeline that's been attacked by three independent reviews, the omission-vs-commission split, and a defensible upper bound on insured loss for this cell on τ² airline.
- **Doesn't:**
  - give a premium
  - give the loss-event ICC (0 events)
  - give severity CV (no insured events; the omission CV² of about 1.8 is used only as a range check)
  - tell us anything about real customer traffic (benchmark tasks are curated, not sampled)
  - generalise beyond Haiku 5.5 with this simulator

## Errors found and fixed
Details are in ITERATIONS.md and REVIEW.md.
- **Loop 1:** the integration tests were vacuous (tasks 0–4 move no money) and the replay outputs were blank. Fixed, with non-vacuity guards.
- **Loop 2:** the netted dollar diff scored **missed bookings as payouts**. Rebuilt to classify by transaction. Review 1 also found **gift-card downgrade refunds double-counted** ($10,488 vs $5,244).
- **Loop 3:** a divergence from gold isn't a wrongful act. Added a **policy-compliance layer**.
- **Review 2:** exact-entry matching and ID pairing made a different route, payment method or booking order to the same correct state look like loss, and certificates were double counted. **Dollar diff v3** uses content pairing, net-per-reservation comparison, change attribution against the initial DB and certificate forfeiture. Omission sizes corrected (task 7 $2,440 → $296).
- **Review 3:**
  - the ceiling depends on the target population
  - the ICC proxy included COMMUNICATE-only failures (0.61, vs 0.42 for DB mismatch)
  - the promised bootstrap was never called
  - the "8%" bound wasn't robust (it's ≤ 11.3%)
  - the plumbing batch had been pooled contrary to the plan
  - option B was labelled "frequency" but was pure premium
  - the minimum task count is ρ·eff
  - table errors and "every failure was an omission" were wrong

  All corrected above. 46 tests now cover the dollar diff, the policy layer and the statistics.
